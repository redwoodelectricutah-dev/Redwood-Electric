Services

Power, picture, network, and the rooms they live in.

A clear list of what the shop takes on. Where we have a photograph, it is on this page. Where we do not, we say so instead of inventing one.

01 — Electrical

Residential electrical

Panels, circuits, lighting, and the repairs that keep a house honest. The work is finished when the deadfront sits flat and the directory can be read by someone who was not on the job.

  • Panel makeup and circuit directories
  • New circuits and lighting
  • Troubleshooting that starts at the panel, not the symptom

02 — Home theater

Rooms you sit in, not racks you apologize for

Screens, in-wall displays, soundbars, and a small stack — Denon, a streamer, shade control — placed so the room still looks like a room.

  • Screen and display walls
  • Power and low voltage for the seat and the rack
  • Gear that is labeled for the person who lives there

03 — UniFi & security

Network and cameras as one system

UniFi Dream Machine, switching, and turret cameras. The cabinet gets a patch field with names. The cameras get an aim that matches the walk, the eave, and the corner.

  • UniFi gateways, switches, and Wi-Fi
  • Camera placement and recording
  • Cabinets dressed so the next visit is short

04 — Data racks

Wall-mount racks with a bottom and a top

A rack is not a shelf of leftovers. Patch panel, switch, power, and the UPS have a place, and the cable between them is routed.

  • Wall-mount cabinets
  • Patch panels and labeling
  • UPS and power arranged so nothing is an afterthought

05 — Loxone

Home automation, after the walkthrough

Loxone lighting and control for houses that want one system instead of a drawer of remotes. We do not keep a stock photo for this, and we will not pretend a theater picture is an automation job. The scope starts when we walk the house.

  • Lighting and scene planning
  • Coordination with the electrical rough-in
  • A system the household can live with

06 — Basement finishing

The electrical side of a basement becoming rooms

When a basement turns into a theater, an office, or a suite, the power and the low voltage have to be in the walls before the finish hides them. We do that part. We do not pretend to be the framer.

  • Circuits for new rooms
  • Low voltage paths before the drywall
  • Coordination with whoever is finishing the space
